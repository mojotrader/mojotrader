Read before sizing

What these numbers do not say

Everything above is a backtest read off two TradingView exports. Four things materially affect how the figures should be used.

Commission

The exports were not run on the same cost basis: over the year, Halyard charged $2,924 and QuantFlow V3 charged $0. Every figure on this page re-prices both at $1 per contract per side ($2 round turn), which moves QuantFlow V3 by $1,548 against its raw net profit. Slippage is not modelled anywhere. At 771 round turns and 2.90 contracts a trade, one MNQ tick of slippage per side is $2,236 — 2% of the joined net profit.

Capital base

The joined column here is one $30,000 account trading both strategies' signals side by side — not two separately funded accounts summed. Same trades, same contract sizes, smaller denominator: net profit is unchanged in dollars but every percentage figure (return, drawdown %, worst day %) is now measured against $30,000 instead of the strategies' combined tester capital, so it reads as more leveraged. Check the margin note below before sizing this for real.

Tail risk

On $30,000 of capital, the book peaks at 11 concurrent contracts ($618,292 notional) open at once — 20.6× the account in notional. That is what your broker's intraday margin has to cover, not the 2.90-contract average; check it against your actual margin schedule before running this live, since a margin call would force an exit neither strategy chose. The joined book's worst day here was -$2,898, 9.7% of $30,000 — and that figure, like max drawdown, is measured on closed-trade equity, so it understates what the account saw while positions were open.

Sample

One year, one instrument, one regime — a strongly trending Nasdaq. 771 trades is a healthy sample for trade-level statistics, but the equity curve rests on only 13 months, so the Sharpe, Calmar and drawdown figures carry far less certainty than their decimal places suggest. No walk-forward or out-of-sample split is present in these exports, and both strategies were tuned on this same window.
